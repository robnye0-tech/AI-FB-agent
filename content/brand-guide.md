# The AI Shortlist — Brand Guide

## Who this is for
Small business owners and solo entrepreneurs who keep hearing about new AI
tools but don't have time to test them all. They want a trusted, no-hype
source that tells them exactly what to use and why — not a firehose of
every tool launch.

## Voice
- Plain, direct, practical. Write like a sharp friend giving advice, not a
  tech blogger.
- No hype words ("game-changing", "revolutionary", "insane"). If a tool is
  mediocre, say so.
- Always answer "why should a solo/small-team business owner care?" — tie
  every tool back to a real task (invoicing, customer emails, content,
  scheduling, bookkeeping, etc.), never just "cool AI thing."
- Faceless brand: no personal anecdotes, no "I tried this today." Speak as
  "The AI Shortlist," a curator, not an individual influencer.

## Content pillars (rotate across these)
1. **Tool Spotlight** — one tool, one specific use case, in depth.
2. **Head-to-Head** — two tools compared for one job (e.g. "Notion AI vs
   ChatGPT for meeting notes").
3. **Shortlist** — "Top 5 AI tools for [task]" roundup.
4. **Mistake to Avoid** — a common AI-adoption mistake small businesses
   make and the fix.
5. **Free vs Paid** — what you can get done with free tiers before paying.

## Visual style (for carousel slide briefs)
- Clean, high-contrast, minimal — dark background, one accent color, large
  type. No stock photos of robots or glowing brains.
- Slide 1 is always a hook question or bold claim, not a title card.
- Last slide is always a CTA: "Save this for later" / "Follow for weekly
  AI shortlists."

## What we never do
- Never claim a tool is "the best" without a stated reason.
- Never recommend a tool the brand hasn't described a concrete use case
  for.
- Never use filler hashtags unrelated to AI/small business/productivity.
- Never recommend a tool more favorably, or more often, because it has an
  affiliate link. The pick comes first; the link is just how you act on it.

## Affiliate disclosure
The page monetizes through affiliate links (see
`content/data/affiliate-links.json` and `content/link-in-bio.md`). Every
post that uses a live affiliate link must say so plainly in the caption —
not just in a hashtag — per the `disclosure_rule` in that file. This is an
FTC requirement, not a style choice, and it applies even though the brand
voice is otherwise unfiltered — disclosure text doesn't get cut for
brevity.
